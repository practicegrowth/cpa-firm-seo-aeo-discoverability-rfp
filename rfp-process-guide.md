# RFP Process Guide — How to Run a Vendor Selection Process

> This guide walks you through the complete RFP process: from identifying vendors to signing a contract. Use this alongside the RFP template and evaluation scorecard.

---

## Phase 1: Preparation (Week 1-2)

### 1.1 Define Your Requirements

Before you start, answer these questions:

- **What are your goals?** (e.g., increase organic traffic by 50%, appear in AI search, improve local visibility)
- **What's your budget?** (Monthly retainer + one-time setup costs)
- **What's your timeline?** (When do you need results?)
- **What are your non-negotiables?** (e.g., must have CPA experience, must optimize for AI search)
- **Who's on the selection committee?** (Managing partner, marketing manager, IT lead)

### 1.2 Identify Potential Vendors

**Where to find vendors:**

- **Industry referrals** — Ask other CPA firms, state CPA society, AICPA
- **Professional directories** — Clutch, UpCity, DesignRush (filter for accounting/CPA experience)
- **Content marketing** — Search "CPA firm SEO" and see who's ranking (they should be)
- **Conference sponsors** — Check AICPA ENGAGE, state society events
- **LinkedIn** — Search "CPA marketing" or "accounting firm SEO"

**Create a shortlist of 5-7 vendors.** You'll send the RFP to all of them, but expect 3-5 quality responses.

### 1.3 Customize the RFP

Use [`rfp-template.md`](rfp-template.md) as your base. Customize:

- **Section 1** — Fill in your firm's specific goals and challenges
- **Section 2** — Complete your firm background (be honest about current state)
- **Section 3** — Mark priority levels for each scope category
- **Section 7** — Set realistic timeline expectations
- **Section 8** — Define your budget range (or ask vendors to propose)
- **Section 11** — Set submission deadline (give vendors 2-3 weeks)

### 1.4 Distribute the RFP

**Send to all shortlisted vendors:**

- Email the RFP template + evaluation scorecard
- Include a cover email with:
  - Brief intro about your firm
  - RFP submission deadline
  - Contact person for questions
  - Expected decision timeline

**Example email:**

```
Subject: SEO & Discoverability RFP — [Your Firm Name]

Hi [Vendor Name],

[Firm Name] is a [size] CPA firm in [location] seeking an SEO and discoverability partner to improve our online visibility across traditional search, AI-powered search, and local results.

We've attached our RFP template and evaluation scorecard. Please review and submit your proposal by [deadline].

We expect to make a decision by [decision date] and begin the engagement by [start date].

Please direct any questions to [contact person] at [email/phone].

We look forward to your response.

Best,
[Your name]
```

## Phase 2: Evaluation (Week 3-4)

### 2.1 Collect and Organize Proposals

**As proposals come in:**

- Acknowledge receipt within 24 hours
- Organize proposals in a shared folder (Google Drive, Dropbox)
- Create a comparison spreadsheet with key data points

### 2.2 Score Proposals

**Use the [`evaluation-scorecard.md`](evaluation-scorecard.md):**

- Each selection committee member scores independently
- Use the 1-5 rubric for each criterion
- Multiply by weights to get final scores
- Schedule a meeting to discuss scores and reach consensus

**Scoring tips:**

- **Look for specificity** — Generic proposals get low scores
- **Demand evidence** — "We do AEO" is not enough. Ask HOW and for PROOF
- **Check for AI-washing** — Vendors claiming AEO but can't explain structured data
- **Verify CPA experience** — Ask for case studies with measurable results
- **Assess transparency** — If they're vague about methodology, that's a red flag

### 2.3 Identify Red Flags 🚩

**Disqualify vendors who:**

- Guarantee #1 rankings (no one can guarantee this)
- Use PBNs or link schemes (ask directly)
- Can't explain AEO or AI search optimization (it's 2026)
- Have no CPA/accounting firm experience
- Provide generic, templated proposals
- Can't provide references from similar firms
- Are vague about pricing or have hidden fees
- Don't understand professional services regulations
- Suggest keyword stuffing or black-hat tactics
- Can't explain their reporting methodology

### 2.4 Shortlist Top 3 Vendors

**Based on scores, select top 3 for presentations:**

- Invite each vendor for a 30-45 minute presentation
- Provide presentation guidelines (what you want them to cover)
- Schedule back-to-back on the same day for easy comparison

## Phase 3: Presentations (Week 5)

### 3.1 Presentation Format

**Ask vendors to cover:**

1. **Agency overview** (5 min) — Who they are, team, relevant experience
2. **Proposed approach** (15 min) — How they'll solve your specific challenges
3. **Case studies** (10 min) — Results from similar CPA firms
4. **Q&A** (10-15 min) — Use [`presentation-interview-questions.md`](presentation-interview-questions.md)

### 3.2 Evaluation During Presentations

**Score vendors on:**

- **Clarity** — Did they explain complex concepts clearly?
- **Specificity** — Did they tailor to your firm or give generic pitch?
- **Expertise** — Did they demonstrate deep knowledge?
- **Cultural fit** — Would you enjoy working with them?
- **Responsiveness** — Did they answer questions directly or deflect?

### 3.3 Reference Checks

**After presentations, contact references:**

- Ask for 2-3 references from each vendor (CPA firms preferred)
- Call (don't email) references
- Ask specific questions:
  - What results did you see?
  - How was communication?
  - Were there any surprises?
  - Would you hire them again?
  - What would you do differently?

---

## Phase 4: Selection & Contract (Week 6)

### 4.1 Final Decision

**Consider:**

- Evaluation scorecard scores
- Presentation quality
- Reference feedback
- Cultural fit
- Pricing and contract terms
- Gut feeling

### 4.2 Negotiate Contract

**Use [`contract-checklist.md`](contract-checklist.md) to ensure:**

- Clear scope of work
- Defined deliverables and timelines
- Pricing and payment terms
- IP ownership (you own everything they create)
- Termination clauses (no long-term lock-in)
- Data handling and confidentiality
- SLAs and performance guarantees

### 4.3 Onboard the Vendor

**Once contract is signed:**

- Schedule kickoff meeting
- Provide access to necessary systems (CMS, analytics, GBP)
- Share brand guidelines, messaging, target personas
- Set communication cadence (weekly calls, monthly reports)
- Establish baseline metrics

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

❌ **Rushing the process** — Don't skip reference checks or presentations
❌ **Choosing on price alone** — Cheap vendors cost more in the long run
❌ **Ignoring AI search** — If they can't do AEO, they're behind the curve
❌ **Not defining success metrics** — How will you know if they're delivering?
❌ **Skipping the contract** — Get everything in writing
❌ **Micromanaging** — Hire experts, then let them work
❌ **Expecting instant results** — SEO takes 6-12 months to show significant results
